Sedum
Table Lamp

Year
2023
Role
Designer, Project Manager, Producer
Scope
Physical Product Project Mgmt Fabrication Prototyping
Overview

A handmade table lamp designed to explore the relationship between industrial process and domestic warmth. The project ran from first sketch to a small production run of twelve.

Sedum began as a personal project with a clear constraint: produce a functional lighting object using only processes available to a single maker working without a factory. The design had to be honest about what it was made of and how it was made, while still meeting the standards of something you'd want to live with.

Problem

Most small-batch lighting objects fall into one of two categories: pure craft pieces that ignore manufacturability, or production objects that have had the life designed out of them in pursuit of consistency. The challenge was finding a third path where the making process itself became part of the visual language.

Managing a project like this without a team or a budget for mistakes required building a very clear decision framework early: what could be iterated cheaply, what had to be right the first time, and what could wait until the design was proven.

Process

Material exploration: spun aluminum, walnut, brass

Three rounds of prototyping, each focused on a different variable: first the shade geometry, then the stem-to-base connection, then the electrical routing. Keeping the variables isolated meant each round produced a clear answer rather than a confusing mix of good and bad decisions.

Vendor coordination involved sourcing a metal spinner in New Jersey, a woodturner in upstate New York, and electrical components from two separate suppliers. Managing lead times across those three relationships while keeping the final assembly sequence logical was the core project management challenge.

Outcome
Final product photograph

Sedum Table Lamp, edition of twelve, 2023

Twelve lamps were produced and all sold within six weeks through direct outreach and one pop-up. The production process was documented in enough detail to run a second edition without re-solving any of the same problems. Material cost per unit came in 14% under the initial estimate after renegotiating the shade specification with the metal spinner.

The most useful decision made early was to treat the project as a process design problem first and a product design problem second. Getting the sequence of operations right made everything downstream easier.
